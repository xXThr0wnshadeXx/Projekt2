# Glance — Business Model, Validation & Pitch Playbook

> Goal: walk into judging with **evidence, not opinions** — real conversations, real numbers, a bottom-up market size, and a pilot path a buyer has actually reacted to.
>
> Built from the Saturday workshops (customer development / Mom Test; OpenAI go-to-market talk) and applied to Glance.

---

## 0. The rules we follow

1. **Start with the problem, not the product.** Every conversation is about their last week, not our app.
2. **Opinions are noise. Stories, money, time, and workarounds are signal.**
3. **Count real buyers × price.** Never "1% of a billion-dollar market."
4. **Go narrow.** Validate with a tiny, specific segment before claiming anything bigger.
5. **Write pass/fail thresholds before we test** (§4). No moving goalposts.
6. **Honesty:** never invent users, quotes, or numbers. Label every hypothesis as a hypothesis. Personas in the demo are "for example." Every stat on a slide has a source.
7. **Outcome over AI.** Lead with time saved and access gained; the AI is how, not why.

## 1. Who we're selling to (narrow)

### Users (who feels the pain)
**Early-adopter student:** a blind or low-vision student in an algebra → calculus course who
- hits inaccessible math **every week** (slides, PDFs, worksheets, board),
- has **already built a workaround** (asks a classmate/reader, waits for alt-format, uses a screen-reader + guesswork, Equatio/Seeing AI),
- would **forgive a rough v1** if it saves them waiting.

### Buyer (who pays)
**Early-adopter buyer:** the **alternate-format / accessibility coordinator** at a public university disability office who
- converts STEM materials **every week of the term**,
- is **already paying** for transcription (staff time, contractors) and missing turnaround targets,
- is under institutional pressure on digital accessibility.

### Secondary (later)
- Teachers of students with visual impairments (K–12 TVIs) and districts.
- Students with motor/handwriting disabilities (voice scratchpad).

## 3. Hypotheses we must test this weekend

| # | Hypothesis | How we test | Pass threshold (set now) |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Blind/low-vision STEM students lose days waiting for accessible math | Student interviews | ≥ 3 of 5 describe a specific recent wait ≥ 2 days |
| H2 | Their current workarounds cost time or depend on other people | Student interviews | ≥ 3 of 5 name a workaround they use weekly |
| H3 | Alt-format offices spend significant staff time/money on STEM conversion | Staff interviews | ≥ 1 office gives a concrete time or $ figure per worksheet/term |
| H4 | A buyer would trial a tool that converts in minutes with student confirmation | End-of-interview ask | ≥ 1 written "yes, we'd pilot" (email/LOI) **or** a booked follow-up meeting |
| H5 | Students would use it themselves (not just via the office) | Sign-up page + sessions | ≥ 10 sign-ups with role = student/staff/TVI; ≥ 3 real sessions |
| H6 | Our output is accurate enough to trust with confirmation | Evals | Capture ≥ 85% equivalent-match on slides/typed; ambiguity asked ≥ 80% |

If a hypothesis fails, **say so in the pitch** and what we changed. Judges reward honest learning.

## 4. Customer interviews (Mom Test)

### Ground rules
- Don't mention Glance until the last 2 minutes.
- Ask about **the last time**, not "would you."
- Listen; ask "why?" and "what happened next?"
- End with: *"Who else should I talk to?"*
- Tell them it's a student hackathon project. Collect only what they choose to share. No disability details recorded without consent; log roles, not names.

### Script A — Blind / low-vision student (15 min)
1. "Tell me about the last math or science assignment you did. What format did it come in?"
2. "Walk me through how you read it. What happened when you got to the equations or diagrams?"
3. "How long did it take from getting the assignment to being able to actually work on it?"
4. "What do you do today when the material isn't accessible? Who helps? What does that cost you — time, grades, asking favors?"
5. "How do you write out your work and turn it in? What's the hardest part?"
6. "Have you tried any tools for this? What did you like or hate?" (listen for Equatio, Seeing AI, Be My Eyes, MathType, LaTeX, readers)
7. "If you could change one thing about how math reaches you, what would it be?"
8. *(Last 2 min, optional)* Show the 60-second demo. "What would stop you from using this?"
9. "Who else should I talk to?"

### Script B — Alternate-format / accessibility coordinator (15 min)
1. "How does a STEM course material request get handled today, from request to delivery?"
2. "Tell me about the last math-heavy request. How many pages? How long did it take? Who did the work?"
3. "Roughly what does that cost — staff hours, contractor fees — per worksheet, per course, per term?"
4. "Where does it break — handwriting, slides, last-minute handouts, exams?"
5. "What tools do you use now? What do they not do?"
6. "What would have to be true for you to trust a tool's output for a student?"
7. "How do you buy software like this — who approves it, what budget, how long does it take?"
8. *(Last 3 min)* Demo. "Would you be open to a free pilot next term with one course? What would success look like?"
9. "Who else should I talk to?"

### Script C — Instructor / TVI (10 min)
1. "When a student needs accessible math in your course, what happens?"
2. "How do you receive work from students who can't hand-write it?"
3. "Would a typed export of the student's own steps work for grading? What would worry you?" (integrity)

### Noise vs. signal
| Noise (don't count) | Signal (count it) |
|---|---|
| "That's cool." "I'd totally use that." | "Last Tuesday I waited 4 days for a problem set." |
| "Sounds useful for someone." | "We spend about X hours per calculus worksheet." |
| Compliments on the demo | A workaround they already pay for or depend on |
| | Booking a follow-up, introducing someone, signing up, agreeing to a pilot in writing |

## 5. Four market tests to run this weekend

1. **Sign-up / waitlist page** (live by Sat evening)
   - Fields: email, role (student / disability-services staff / instructor / TVI / other), "What's the hardest part of math accessibility for you?" (free text).
   - Track: visits, sign-ups by role. Share **one-to-one** (DMs, specific community posts asking for advice) — no mass emails.
2. **Concierge conversion (manual delivery)**
   - Offer to convert a real worksheet/slide for a student or office **today**. Run it through Glance, have a teammate double-check, deliver. Time it end to end.
   - Evidence: "Converted N real pages in M minutes vs. their usual X days."
3. **Written pilot commitment (no money)**
   - Ask an office/instructor for a short email: "We'd be open to piloting Glance with one course next term if…" That's a **letter of intent**. Screenshot with permission.
4. **Real usage sessions**
   - 15-min remote sessions; log what worked/broke; ask permission to quote.

Don't take payments without checking with the help desk first.

## 7. Market size — bottom-up only

**Formula:** reachable buyers × price × adoption you can defend.

### Step 1 — Count buyers we can name
- **Beachhead:** CSU system — 22 universities. Each has a disability services office.
- **Next:** other public universities (UC, community colleges, out-of-state publics) — count from public lists.
- **Later:** K–12 districts with registered blind/visually impaired students (APH census by state).

### Step 2 — Price (hypothesis until an interview confirms)
- Anchor to what buyers already spend (transcription staff time, contractors, per-page costs).
- Ask every coordinator: *"What do you spend per term on STEM conversion?"* Put their answer in the ledger.
- Pricing model to test: **annual campus license, free for students**, priced well below current conversion spend for the same volume.

### Step 3 — Show the math with real inputs
```
Year-1 SAM = (# CSU campuses that agree to pilot) × (license price validated in interviews)
Expansion  = (# public universities in CA/US) × price × conservative adoption %
```
Only fill in numbers we can source or that a buyer told us. If price isn't validated, show the formula and say "price under validation — interview data so far: …".

## 8. Business model

| Element | Glance |
|---|---|
| **Customer segments** | Disability services / alt-format offices (buyer); blind, low-vision, motor-impaired STEM students (user); TVIs / districts (later) |
| **Value proposition** | Same-day accessible math from real materials (photos, scans, slides); students do and hand in their own work; no-solve modes instructors can rely on |
| **Channels** | Direct to disability offices (start: Cal Poly → CSU); accessibility conferences/networks; student blindness organizations; instructor referrals |
| **Customer relationships** | Free pilot with a written success plan → annual license; student feedback loop |
| **Revenue** | Annual campus license (students free); district license later |
| **Key activities** | Accuracy evals, accessibility QA, onboarding pilots |
| **Key resources** | Capture + verification pipeline, accessibility-first UX, eval datasets |
| **Key partners** | Disability offices, OpenAI API, open-source math accessibility (MathJax/SRE) |
| **Cost structure** | Model API usage per page/line, hosting, support |

### Unit economics (measure, don't guess)
- From evals, log tokens/cost per capture and per spoken line.
- Report: **cost per page converted** and **cost per student per term** (estimate pages/term from interviews).
- Compare to the buyer's current cost per page/term from interviews.

## 9. Pilot design (borrowed from enterprise sales: proof of concept + mutual success plan)

One-page pilot plan we can send a disability office:

```
Pilot: Glance for <Course>, <Term>
Scope: 1 course, 1–3 students, all math handouts/slides
Success criteria (agreed up front):
  - Turnaround: material usable by the student within 1 hour of release (vs. current __ days)
  - Accuracy: ≥ __% of lines correct before student confirmation; 100% student-confirmed
  - Student satisfaction: ≥ 4/5 on usability
  - Staff time saved: __ hours vs. last term
Commitments: Glance provides onboarding + weekly check-in; office provides materials + feedback
Decision: if criteria met → annual license proposal
```

Getting even a reply that says "send this over" is evidence.

## 10. Competition and why they'd switch

| Alternative | Why someone uses it | Why they'd switch to Glance (only claim what we verified) |
|---|---|---|
| Human transcription / alt-format staff | Accurate | Days–weeks vs. minutes; frees staff for hard cases |
| Equatio (screenshot reader, speech input) | Licensed widely; teacher-friendly editor | Test hands-on (ticket #35). Candidate differences: blind-first whole-page input, ambiguity-aware voice, verified steps, confirm-every-line hand-in workspace |
| General AI chatbots | Free, fast | Solve answers (not allowed in coursework); output not navigable math; can misread silently |
| Seeing AI / Be My Eyes | Great general description | Not navigable/verifiable math, no workspace |
| MathJax/MathCAT in documents | Excellent when authored correctly | Most real materials aren't authored as MathML |
