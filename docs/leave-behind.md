# Glance

Glance lets blind and low-vision students understand any page of maths by voice, on their own. It reads the page, helps them grasp its shape and parts, and writes down their work. It never does the work for them.

## Who it's for

Blind and low-vision students taking algebra-level maths, and the disability-services teams and teachers of students with visual impairments who support them.

## What gets in the way

A worksheet can arrive as a photo or scan. A student may then have to wait for a converted copy or arrange for someone to read and write with them. Stanford's in-house alternate-format team targets **4–5 business days**. A historical report put the cost of producing one braille maths textbook at **up to $12,000**. [Stanford](https://oae.stanford.edu/faculty-staff/alternate-format-production) · [The Chronicle of Higher Education](https://www.chronicle.com/article/purdue-makes-math-and-science-materials-for-the-blind-available-on-line/)

## What changes

Start with the material the class received. Hear the shape of the expression before reading its parts, then dictate or type your own work, confirm the readback, and hand in typed maths that an instructor can read.

## Why it's trusted

Glance reads and records the student's own work. It asks when dictation is ambiguous and gives no answers, hints, or next steps. Exam mode turns off step checks. The integrity contract tracks enforcement, and the instructor-link change for locking exam mode is under review.
