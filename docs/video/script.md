# Narration (≈ 230 words, ~1:45 at a steady pace)

Read slowly; leave the screen reader room to speak. Replace the bracketed
parts only with things we can show or source.

**1. (0:00)** Math assumes you can see it and hold a pencil. For blind and low-vision students, that's the barrier — not ability. This is Glance.

**2. (0:10)** A student uploads a worksheet. Glance reads it into real, structured math, and moves straight to the first line.

**3. (0:25)** Before the details, the shape: "an equation; on the left, two terms." Then they explore it piece by piece with the arrow keys.

**4. (0:45)** Now the work. They hold Space and say their step. Glance reads it back, and nothing is saved until they confirm it.

**5. (1:00)** In homework mode, algebra software checks the step. Glance never solves the problem, and never says "wrong" — only that a step checks out, or that it couldn't confirm it.

**6. (1:10)** Spoken math is ambiguous. "x plus one over two" could mean two things, so Glance asks instead of guessing.

**7. (1:25)** Low-vision students can read one line at a time, as large as they need.

**8. (1:35)** When they're done, they hand in typed work: an accessible file their instructor can read, and their screen reader can too.

**9. (1:45)** Glance. Math you can hear and speak. [Link.]
